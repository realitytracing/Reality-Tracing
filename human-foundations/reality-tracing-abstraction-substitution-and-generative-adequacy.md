# Abstraction, Substitution, and Generative Adequacy

## Purpose

This document clarifies how Reality Tracing treats abstraction.

Abstraction is necessary.

Finite agents cannot carry every feature of reality into every act of reasoning.
We compress.

We group.

We omit.

We build categories from traces, categories from categories, and models from prior models.

The problem is therefore not abstraction itself.

The problem is allowing an abstraction to perform a task for which it no longer preserves the distinctions that matter.

This document develops a task-relative discipline for detecting that failure.

Its central rule is:

> If two descendants are interchangeable within a category but not interchangeable within the task, the category is too coarse to generate the task.

This is the **Substitution Test**.

The broader principle is:

> Perturb the allegedly irrelevant dimension and see whether the conclusion moves.

If the conclusion moves, the omitted dimension was not irrelevant to that task.

---

## Status

This is a methodological note.

It does not claim:

- that higher abstraction is inherently worse
- that third-degree abstraction is inherently closed
- that every abstraction must preserve every lower-order detail
- that useful abstraction must be reversible
- that all compression is epistemically dangerous
- that every task requires first-degree access
- that every local judgment can be generated by a universal rule
- that higher-order categories are unreal
- that description and prescription are the same operation

It makes a narrower claim:

> Abstraction quality is task-relative.

A category may be:

- useful for description
- useful for navigation
- useful for comparison
- useful for inquiry
- useful for prediction
- useful for one policy question

while remaining too coarse for:

- another inference
- another intervention
- another scope
- another timescale
- another clamp specification
- another normative claim

A concise formulation:

> A valid compression is not automatically a valid generator.

---

# I. Degrees of Abstraction

Reality Tracing uses degrees of abstraction as an **altitude description**, not as a validity ranking.

The degrees describe how far a representation has been constructed from more immediate traces.

They do not, by themselves, tell us whether the abstraction is good or bad.

## 1. First-Degree Trace

A first-degree trace remains close to direct lived, sensed, measured, or observed interaction.

Examples may include:

- pain
- fatigue
- observed temperature
- hunger
- measured acceleration
- a person reporting "I feel trapped"
- a repeated directly observed behavioral regularity
- an instrument reading under declared conditions

First-degree does not mean infallible.

Signals can be noisy, partial, misread, instrument-limited, or frame-limited.

It means the trace remains comparatively close to the interaction being reported.

## 2. Second-Degree Abstraction

A second-degree abstraction organizes first-degree traces into:

- concepts
- categories
- models
- mechanisms
- patterns
- rules
- institutional terms
- scientific constructs
- social interpretations

Examples may include:

- inflation
- trauma
- market concentration
- adulthood
- procedural fairness
- ecosystem stress
- attention bottleneck

Second-degree abstraction is ordinary and necessary.

It makes coordination, explanation, science, and policy possible.

## 3. Third-Degree Abstraction

A third-degree abstraction is built substantially from already-abstracted categories, models, or explanatory structures.

Examples may include:

- objectivity
- rationality
- progress
- capitalism
- empire
- social order
- liberation
- sustainability
- scientific legitimacy
- adulthood as a generalized social ontology
- a theory of what several lower-order frameworks have in common

Third-degree abstraction is not inherently defective.

Expertise often operates at high abstraction.

Mathematics can operate at very high abstraction.

Institutional design requires abstraction over many lower-order systems.

The mistake is to treat abstraction degree as if it already contained the failure diagnosis.

A concise formulation:

> Altitude is not guilt.

Another:

> The question is not how high the abstraction is. The question is what work it is being asked to do after compression.

---

# II. Downward Traceability and Generative Adequacy

Two different tests must be kept separate.

## 1. Downward Traceability

Downward traceability asks:

> Can the higher abstraction still be connected back to the lower-order traces, interactions, measurements, mechanisms, or lived conditions from which it was constructed?

A category may remain downward-traceable even when it is highly abstract.

For example:

> triads and constraint planes  
> → constraint-sensitive objectivity  
> → objectivity

The word "objectivity" may remain perfectly traceable back to the structures that motivated it.

That does not mean "objectivity" can regenerate those structures.

This produces the second test.

## 2. Generative Adequacy

Generative adequacy asks:

> Does the abstraction preserve enough discriminative structure to perform the task now being asked of it?

This is not the same as asking whether the abstraction is true.

A category can be descriptively accurate while remaining generatively insufficient.

A concise formulation:

> Traceability asks whether the stairs still go down.  
> Generative adequacy asks whether enough machinery survived the trip up.

---

# III. Descriptive–Generative Asymmetry

A higher-order abstraction may correctly describe what several lower-order structures have in common while failing to preserve enough information to reconstruct the distinctions that made those structures work.

This is the **Descriptive–Generative Asymmetry**.

A concise formulation:

> What can summarize a system need not be able to rebuild it.

Consider a family of constraint-aware tests.

A higher abstraction may correctly describe them as:

> objective

But "objective" may also describe many other procedures:

- empirical measurement
- cost-benefit analysis
- mathematical optimization
- procedural neutrality
- majority aggregation
- causal prediction
- technocratic expert judgment
- metric maximization

Some may preserve the original function.

Some may not.

Therefore:

> Triads → "objective"

may be descriptively legitimate.

But:

> "objective" → triads

does not follow.

The category underdetermines its descendants.

The failure begins when the higher abstraction is treated as though the lost distinctions were still present.

---

# IV. Generative Loss

**Generative loss** is the loss of information during abstraction that matters to the task the abstraction is later asked to perform.

Not all information loss is a problem.

A subway map omits:

- building colors
- tree species
- sidewalk textures
- restaurant menus

That loss may be irrelevant to the task of choosing a train route.

But if the same map omits:

- which lines are closed
- which stations are inaccessible
- where service terminates

the lost information may change the required decision.

The important question is therefore not:

> Did abstraction lose information?

Almost all useful abstraction does.

The important question is:

> Did it lose information that could change the answer to the current task?

This may be called **decision-relevant generative loss**.

A concise formulation:

> Loss becomes dangerous when the discarded distinction could change the required operation.

---

# V. The Substitution Test

The Substitution Test is a lightweight diagnostic for task-relative abstraction failure.

Ask:

> If I replace one member of this category with another member while keeping the higher category true, can the inference, judgment, clamp, or operation I care about materially change?

If yes, the category is too coarse to author that result by itself.

Let:

- \(A\) be an abstraction
- \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) be lower-order cases compatible with \(A\)
- \(J(x)\) be the judgment, inference, policy, action, or specification required for the task

If:

\[
x_1 \in A
\]

and:

\[
x_2 \in A
\]

but:

\[
J(x_1) \neq J(x_2)
\]

then:

\[
A \nRightarrow J
\]

This notation is shorthand for the structural point.

It is not a claim that every philosophical or policy judgment can be fully formalized.

The conclusion is simple:

> The abstraction cannot see a distinction that the task still needs.

---

# VI. Three Task-Relative Abstraction States

The Substitution Test produces three useful states.

## 1. Generatively Usable

The abstraction remains stable enough under relevant substitution for the task.

The distinctions it discards do not materially alter the required operation under the declared scope and conditions.

This does not mean the abstraction is universally lossless.

It means it is **lossless enough for the task**.

## 2. Lossy but Legitimate

The abstraction is unstable under substitution for a particular task, but is being used only for:

- description
- grouping
- communication
- navigation
- comparison
- indexing
- inquiry setup

This is not a failure.

The category may remain extremely useful.

A folder label does not need to contain the files.

## 3. Generatively Illegitimate Use

The abstraction is unstable under substitution for the task, yet is allowed to dictate:

- a judgment
- a prescription
- a clamp thickness
- a policy
- a constitutive claim
- a normative rule
- an institutional mandate

The error lies in the **use**, not necessarily in the existence of the abstraction.

A concise formulation:

> A category may remain valid as metadata while becoming invalid as source code.

---

# VII. Generative Substitution

**Generative Substitution** occurs when a compressed abstraction is treated as though it can replace the richer lower-order machinery from which it was derived.

The pattern is:

> rich trace  
> → useful compression  
> → compression gains authority  
> → compression is treated as generator  
> → lost distinctions disappear from judgment

Example:

> constraint-sensitive triads  
> → objectivity  
> → "therefore maximize the measurable variable"

The word inherited authority from the lower machinery without preserving the machinery.

Generative Substitution does not require malicious intent.

It can arise from:

- convenience
- elegance
- time pressure
- ideological habit
- institutional shorthand
- excessive confidence
- salience reward from unification
- genuine misunderstanding

A concise formulation:

> The folder label begins pretending it contains the executable.

---

# VIII. The Compression Illusion

Compression often feels like understanding.

A thinker discovers that several successful mechanisms share a common structure.

This creates a legitimate coherence gain.

But coherence gain can be mistaken for information preservation.

The mind moves from:

> These things share property X.

to:

> X contains what makes these things work.

That second claim does not automatically follow.

This is the **Compression Illusion**.

A concise formulation:

> Coherence gain is not proof of generative sufficiency.

Another:

> A theory can become prettier while its eyesight gets worse.

The framework should therefore ask after every major compression:

- What disappeared?
- Which distinctions were merged?
- Could any merged distinction change the task?
- What authority is the new category being granted?
- Is that authority larger than the information retained?

---

# IX. Answer-Generative and Inquiry-Generative Adequacy

Generative adequacy should be divided into at least two forms.

## 1. Answer-Generative Adequacy

An abstraction has answer-generative adequacy when it preserves enough information to generate the required answer under the declared task, scope, and conditions.

Some abstractions can do this.

Many cannot do it universally.

A domain model may generate a useful answer for a tightly bounded domain while remaining insufficient outside it.

## 2. Inquiry-Generative Adequacy

An abstraction has inquiry-generative adequacy when it preserves enough structure to regenerate the questions needed to construct the answer responsibly.

This is especially important for framework-level methods.

A reusable method may be able to ask:

- What is the relevant constraint plane?
- What function or dependency is being supported?
- What is the load?
- What happens when the clamp is too thin?
- What happens when it is too thick?
- What scope and timescale matter?
- What would revise the model?

without being able to answer those questions in advance.

A concise formulation:

> A higher-order abstraction need not preserve the lower-order answer, but it should preserve enough structure to recover the inquiry that made the answer warranted.

This is why a reusable specification discipline can be valuable without becoming a universal answer generator.

---

# X. Protocol Default, Specification Earned

A higher-order protocol may universalize questions further than it can universalize answers.

The valid form is:

\[
\text{Protocol} + \text{Local Trace} \rightarrow \text{Specification}
\]

The invalid shortcut is:

\[
\text{Protocol} \rightarrow \text{Specification}
\]

The missing input is not decorative context.

It contains variables necessary to distinguish possible descendants.

A concise formulation:

> Protocol default, specification earned.

Another:

> Universalize procedure only as far as its discriminative structure survives. Localize conclusions wherever correctness depends on trace-supplied information.

This principle is developed further in the clamp-specification note.

---

# XI. Trace-Bypass Error

A **Trace-Bypass Error** occurs when a conclusion, specification, clamp, or prescription that requires local trace information is asserted without supplying or preserving the trace that makes the conclusion discriminatively warranted.

The thinker has not merely omitted "context."

They have bypassed the information-producing stage.

A concise formulation:

> Required local variables unresolved.

Several related errors belong under this family.

## 1. Generative Substitution

A higher abstraction replaces lower machinery despite having lost task-relevant distinctions.

## 2. Premature Specification

A local answer is declared before enough trace has been performed.

Example:

> The correct clamp is 12%.

when the relevant:

- capacity
- distribution
- rate
- externalities
- local variation
- path dependence

have not yet been traced.

Premature Specification does not require ideology.

It may simply be impatience.

## 3. Specification Sovereignty

**Specification Sovereignty** is the systemic failure in which an abstraction, doctrine, model, institution, or protocol begins treating itself as the source of variables that reality was supposed to provide.

The model no longer asks reality for the answer.

It overwrites the input.

A concise formulation:

> The framework begins authoring the world it was supposed to trace.

Repeated Trace-Bypass can therefore progress toward:

> trace bypass  
> → normalized bypass  
> → specification sovereignty  
> → dogmatic capture

---

# XII. The Perturbation Principle

The Substitution Test is one member of a broader Reality Tracing operation:

> Perturb the allegedly irrelevant dimension and see whether the conclusion moves.

This can be done in several ways.

## 1. Descendant Substitution

Swap one lower-order member of a category for another.

Ask:

> Does the required judgment change?

## 2. Variable Change

Change a variable the argument treats as secondary or fixed.

Ask:

> Does the conclusion survive?

## 3. Invariant Removal

Remove or vary something the model quietly assumed invariant.

Ask:

> Was the "invariant" actually carrying the result?

## 4. Scope Reslicing

Change the observational boundary.

Ask:

> Does the conclusion change when the system boundary expands, contracts, or shifts?

## 5. Temporal Reslicing

Change the time horizon.

Ask:

> Is this claim true immediately, during transition, after adaptation, in equilibrium, during recovery, or across generations?

A policy may look beneficial at one timescale and harmful at another.

## 6. Externality Expansion

Add effects that were placed outside the original accounting surface.

Ask:

> Does the result survive once shifted burden is included?

The general rule is:

> If the conclusion moves when the supposedly irrelevant dimension moves, that dimension was not irrelevant to the task.

---

# XIII. Constitutive Claims Require Stronger Tests

A common abstraction failure occurs when a regularity becomes a definition.

The sequence may be:

> observed pattern  
> → category description  
> → constitutive claim  
> → normative claim

Each transition requires additional warrant.

A prevalence claim does not automatically establish essence.

A personal report does not automatically establish prevalence.

A category description does not automatically establish obligation.

A concise formulation:

> Experience may establish possibility. Prevalence may establish pattern. Neither alone establishes essence or obligation.

A useful constitutive question is:

> If this feature is removed, does the category cease to be what it is, or does it merely become a different member of the same category?

If adults can remain adults while playing, then:

> adult → must not play

cannot be generated merely by the category "adult."

If emotionally expressive men remain men, then emotional reserve cannot be constitutive of manhood merely because it is socially common.

The category may track a real sociological regularity.

It does not thereby earn constitutive sovereignty.

---

# XIV. Normative Backflow

Abstraction failure can travel in both directions.

The first direction is upward:

> lower-order variation  
> → compression  
> → lost distinctions

The second is downward:

> compressed abstraction  
> → mistaken essence  
> → normative rule  
> → behavioral pressure  
> → altered lower-order traces

This is **Normative Backflow**.

The abstraction begins restructuring the cases it originally summarized.

A self-reinforcing loop may form:

> constrained social pattern  
> → abstraction  
> → "this is what the category is"  
> → "this is what members should do"  
> → enforcement  
> → more members resemble the abstraction  
> → apparent confirmation

The sociology begins manufacturing evidence for its own ontology.

A concise formulation:

> The abstraction stops merely describing the equilibrium and begins helping produce it.

This is especially important in:

- adulthood
- gender
- class
- institutional identity
- professional identity
- national identity
- political enemy categories
- moral role categories

---

# XV. False Constraints as a Downstream Case

A **false constraint** may be treated, in this framework, as a phenomenologically operative limit, requirement, prohibition, or necessity whose authoring abstraction fails the Substitution Test.

Example:

> "I am an adult, therefore I should not play."

The constraint may be psychologically real.

It may change:

- behavior
- embarrassment
- opportunity
- salience
- action space

What is false is not necessarily the causal effect.

What is false is the claimed necessity supplied by the abstraction.

This distinction matters.

Two people may obey the same false constraint:

- one may independently prefer the same action
- another may be pushed away from a preferred viable equilibrium

Therefore:

> False-constraint status is determined by the claimed authoring relation.  
> Severity is determined by interaction with the local system.

A concise formulation:

> Subjectively operative does not imply objectively warranted.

Removing a false constraint also does not justify the opposite norm.

The framework should not replace:

> adults must not play

with:

> liberated adults must play

The proper result is:

> adulthood alone does not determine the judgment; return to the local trace.

---

# XVI. The Opposite-Property Fallacy

Another common generative failure appears when a failed system is abstracted into one disliked property and the proposed alternative is treated as superior merely because it has the opposite property.

The pattern is:

> system A failed  
> → A has property X  
> → therefore not-X is better

Examples:

- centralized failed → decentralized is better
- inflationary failed → fixed supply is better
- discretionary failed → no discretion is better
- rigid failed → maximally flexible is better
- underregulated failed → total regulation is better

The opposite property may remove one failure while generating another.

A concise formulation:

> The opposite of a broken system is not automatically a working system.

Another:

> Difference is not adequacy.

The replacement must be independently traced against:

- function
- capacity
- burden
- rate
- externalities
- distribution
- failure modes
- correction pathways
- local-end viability
- environmental constraint

The absence of the old harm does not prove the presence of a viable system.

---

# XVII. Master-Abstraction Routing

A high-level abstraction can be causally relevant and still become too coarse when every lower-order problem is routed through it.

Examples may include:

- capitalism
- empire
- patriarchy
- civilization
- markets
- bureaucracy
- technology
- culture
- biology

The problem is not using these categories.

The problem appears when the category becomes the mandatory explanatory destination for every lower-order trace.

A person may be:

- lonely
- exhausted
- materially constrained
- socially isolated
- institutionally excluded

and a higher-order system may contribute to those conditions.

But intervention still requires distinguishing:

- which pathway
- which scale
- which mechanism
- which local condition
- which reversible lever
- which immediate need

A concise formulation:

> A true macro-explanation can still be too coarse to repair the Tuesday afternoon.

Master abstractions should therefore remain traceable through intermediate mechanisms rather than functioning as universal blame sinks.

---

# XVIII. Scoreboards and Compression

A high-level metric may remain valid while becoming too coarse to stand in for the lived system it summarizes.

Examples may include:

- aggregate growth
- market indices
- average income
- institutional performance scores
- public approval
- productivity
- engagement

A scoreboard can accurately report one slice.

The error begins when:

> the scoreboard is treated as the floor.

A concise formulation:

> A valid metric is not automatically a valid substitute for the system it measures.

This is another form of task-relative abstraction.

The metric may be excellent for:

- comparison
- trend detection
- one narrow decision

while remaining insufficient for:

- burden distribution
- household viability
- ecological sustainability
- local-end stability
- hidden externalities

The proper response is not anti-metric.

It is reslicing.

---

# XIX. High-Salience Semantic Abstractions

Some terms are especially vulnerable to generative overreach because they remain anchored in powerful first-degree human salience while also functioning as high-order public abstractions.

Examples may include:

- freedom
- justice
- peace
- fairness
- dignity
- equality
- safety
- security
- liberation
- rights
- oppression
- order

A person may experience:

> I feel trapped.

That first-degree report deserves attention.

But the institutional claim:

> This policy increases freedom.

has moved into a different epistemic role.

It must declare the slice.

Questions include:

- freedom for whom?
- from what?
- toward what?
- by what mechanism?
- at what cost?
- who bears the burden?
- what rival local ends are compressed?
- what would count as failure?
- what would trigger revision?

A concise formulation:

> First-degree invocation deserves listening. Core 2 invocation owes accounting.

Another:

> Infinite aspiration may be human. Infinite mandate is not.

These semantic cases are special applications of the same abstraction discipline.

The moral force of the lower signal cannot be automatically inherited by the higher mandate.

---

# XX. Abstraction Does Not Become Invalid Because It Cannot Execute

A crucial anti-error rule:

> Failure of answer-generative adequacy does not erase descriptive usefulness.

If "objective" is too coarse to generate a policy, it may still be a useful category.

If "adult" is too coarse to determine whether someone should play, it remains useful for many other purposes.

If "capitalism" is too coarse to specify an intervention, it may remain useful for historical, institutional, or economic analysis.

If an aggregate metric cannot describe household life, it may still remain useful as an aggregate metric.

The framework therefore does not destroy abstractions merely because they fail one task.

It reassigns their jurisdiction.

A concise formulation:

> Do not delete the folder because it cannot run the program.

---

# XXI. Relationship to Clamp Modeling

Clamp modeling asks:

- what appears to continue without bound?
- does reality continue that way?
- if not, what stops, shapes, redirects, saturates, or transforms the process?
- under what conditions does the stopping structure fail?

Abstraction discipline asks a prior and parallel question:

> Does the category being used to model the process still preserve the distinctions necessary to answer those questions?

This prevents a clamp from being flattened into:

- balance
- moderation
- boundedness
- sustainability
- constraint
- enough

and then treated as if the label could generate the local clamp.

Those words may describe successful clamps.

They do not automatically contain the information needed to specify them.

A concise formulation:

> Clamp modeling finds the missing bound. Abstraction discipline checks whether the language used to name the bound still preserves the task.

---

# XXII. Relationship to Science

Scientific abstractions are often highly compressed.

Their strength comes from:

- disciplined derivation
- measurement
- prediction
- replication
- failure exposure
- scope control
- recontact pathways

A scientific model may therefore have strong generative adequacy for one Y-axis scientific task.

But that does not automatically grant answer-generative adequacy for a wider X-axis governance task.

If two real-world situations satisfy the same scientific finding while requiring different policies because of:

- burden
- local-end capacity
- infrastructure
- transition cost
- enforcement
- social trust
- environmental interaction

then the scientific result cannot by itself author the policy.

This does not weaken the science.

It correctly limits the jurisdiction of the abstraction.

A concise formulation:

> Epistemic strength inside a slice does not automatically settle translation outside the slice.

---

# XXIII. Relationship to Objecthood

The Substitution Test does not decide objecthood by itself.

A category may fail to generate a task and still track a robust object.

An object may also require different judgments across contexts.

Objecthood concerns the strength with which a structure continues to force reencounter across:

- scopes
- methods
- frames
- theories
- refinements

Generative adequacy concerns whether the representation preserves enough information for a declared operation.

These questions must not be fused.

A concise formulation:

> Objecthood asks whether reality keeps making us meet the structure. Generative adequacy asks whether our current abstraction of that structure can do the requested work.

---

# XXIV. Self-Application

Reality Tracing must remain vulnerable to its own diagnostic.

The phrase:

> Reality Tracing compatible

cannot substitute for:

- triad inspection
- constraint-plane analysis
- scope declaration
- temporal reslicing
- burden accounting
- externality tracing
- local specification
- revision pathways

If two very different policies can both be described as "Reality Tracing compatible" while requiring different judgments under the framework's own tools, then the label cannot author the judgment.

A concise formulation:

> The framework name is metadata, not a warrant.

Another:

> Cool sticker. Run the trace.

A framework that cannot survive substitution against itself has become the kind of abstraction it was built to audit.

---

# XXV. Field Diagnostic

When a high-level category begins doing suspiciously much work, ask:

1. What task is the abstraction being asked to perform?
2. What lower-order cases fit inside the category?
3. Can I substitute one for another while keeping the category true?
4. Does the required judgment change?
5. What information disappeared in the compression?
6. Was any lost distinction decision-relevant?
7. Can I change scope, time, or a supposed invariant and move the conclusion?
8. Is the abstraction being used descriptively or generatively?
9. Does the conclusion require local trace variables not yet supplied?
10. Is the category beginning to reshape the lower traces through normative backflow?

If the answer reveals task-relevant instability:

> return to the trace.

---

# XXVI. Structural Principle

A concise principle:

> Abstraction is a necessary compression performed by finite agents. Its legitimacy is task-relative. A higher-order category may remain true, useful, and downward-traceable while lacking enough retained structure to generate a particular judgment. When members are interchangeable in the category but not interchangeable in the task, the category is too coarse to author that task.

A sharper formulation:

> Descriptive success does not confer generative authority.

Another:

> A useful abstraction may ignore differences only when those differences are irrelevant to the operation being asked of it.

Another:

> If the conclusion changes when an allegedly irrelevant dimension is perturbed, that dimension was carrying information the abstraction could not safely discard.

And another:

> Protocol default, specification earned.

---

# Final Compression

Finite beings must abstract.

That is not a defect.

Without abstraction there is no tractable thought, science, language, institution, policy, or coordinated action.

But abstraction always risks compression loss.

The important question is not whether information was lost.

The important question is whether the lost information can change the task.

A higher abstraction may accurately summarize many lower-order structures while remaining unable to regenerate the distinctions that made those structures work.

This is Descriptive–Generative Asymmetry.

The resulting loss becomes dangerous when the abstraction is treated as though it retained the lower machinery.

This is Generative Substitution.

The cheap diagnostic is the Substitution Test:

> If two descendants are interchangeable within the category but not interchangeable within the task, the category is too coarse to generate the task.

The wider diagnostic is perturbation:

- substitute descendants
- change variables
- remove supposed invariants
- reslice scope
- reslice time
- expand the externality surface

Then ask whether the conclusion moves.

If it does, the omitted dimension mattered.

This does not mean the abstraction must be discarded.

It may remain useful as:

- description
- metadata
- navigation
- comparison
- inquiry
- communication

The mistake is giving it more jurisdiction than its retained information can support.

That mistake can become a Trace-Bypass Error.

Repeated trace bypass can become Specification Sovereignty.

And when abstractions flow back downward as constitutive or normative rules, they can begin reshaping the traces they once merely described.

Reality Tracing therefore keeps two disciplines alive at once:

> climb high enough to see structure

and:

> return low enough to recover the distinctions the task still needs

The framework points.

The abstraction is tested.

The trace supplies the missing variables.

The case decides.
