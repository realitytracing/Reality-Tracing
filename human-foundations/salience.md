# Salience

## Virtual Energy and Salience (Tractability Clarification)

For the purposes of tractability, the terms **energy**, **salience**, **salience structure**, and **active salience field** in this body of work are virtual modeling constructs.

They are not claims about literal conserved substances, nor are they metaphysical primitives.

**Energy** refers to bounded transformation capacity: the finite ability of a system, whether biological, cognitive, or social, to change state over time.

It is a bookkeeping abstraction used to track how much transformation a system can sustain under constraint.

The "conservation" implied here is structural rather than physical.

Transformation capacity is always limited, always routed, and always paid for in some form, such as metabolic cost, attention, coordination load, opportunity cost, recovery debt, social cost, or material burden, even as it changes substrate across layers.

**Salience** refers to the routing and weighting process that directs bounded transformation capacity toward particular paths rather than allowing it to diffuse across all possible alternatives.

Without salience, transformation would distribute across too many competing possibilities for coherent action, stabilization, or persistence to occur.

Salience constrains combinatorial branching by selectively reinforcing certain trajectories, suppressing others, and making some routes more reachable than others under present constraint.

A useful distinction:

- In ordinary usage, **salience** often means what stands out, grabs attention, or feels prominent.
- In this framework, salience is broader and more structural: it is the bounded routing and weighting process that makes standoutness, repetition, persistence, direction, and coherence possible in finite systems.

Something can be vivid or attention-grabbing without becoming deeply salient in this framework.

Something can also be structurally salient without being flashy, novel, or dramatic.

Salience and energy are therefore virtual tools for modeling:

- bounded capacity
- directed transformation
- stabilization under constraint
- prevention of combinatorial explosion
- internal routing equilibrium
- route-space narrowing and widening
- persistence without total optimization

They do not posit a hidden ontology.

They provide a disciplined way to trace how finite systems persist without dissolving into randomness.

A concise formulation:

> Salience is the bounded routing and weighting of finite capacity into workable paths.

Another:

> Salience is how finite systems make some routes live and others fade.

With this tractability clarification in place, we can now describe how salience operates within bounded systems.

---

## Energy Must Flow Somewhere

Capacity must go somewhere.

In living systems, unused capacity does not remain neutral.

It accumulates pressure, becomes available for rerouting, destabilizes existing local ends, or searches for new sinks.

Salience is the regulatory process by which finite capacity is routed, stabilized, dissipated, redirected, or held in patterned tension over time.

Salience does not act by intention or pure choice.

It emerges as a constraint-bound allocation process that creates or reinforces **ends** into which capacity can sink.

Salience is therefore not preference, motivation, value, attention, or desire in isolation.

It is **capacity stabilization under constraint**.

Energy is a primary capacity, but not the only one salience routes.

Salience may route:

- metabolic energy
- attention
- time
- emotion
- memory
- expectation
- social trust
- cognitive focus
- coordination effort
- recovery capacity
- imagination
- identity pressure

A concise formulation:

> Capacity must flow. Salience gives it a route.

Another:

> Salience is not what a system likes. It is how a system keeps capacity from scattering.

## Salience as Directional Flow

Anything that enters the mind can become an idea.

Any grouping of ideas can itself be treated as an idea.

Sets of ideas can be operationalized into thoughts, movements, habits, interpretations, and local ends.

Salience is the direction of this movement: the ongoing reinforcement of certain ideas, patterns, routes, actions, and interpretations through allocation of finite capacity over time.

Salience determines:

- what receives attention
- what is repeated
- what is resisted
- what is allowed to fade
- what becomes easier to return to
- what remains reachable under current conditions
- what is treated as urgent
- what becomes background
- what can absorb capacity without immediate failure

It operates continuously and automatically.

Conscious reflection can influence salience, but does not suspend it.

Salience is therefore not just about what is currently chosen.

It also concerns what remains **available to be chosen**.

A concise formulation:

> Salience is not only the chosen route. It is the weighted field that makes some routes choosable.

Another:

> What remains reachable is part of what is salient.

## Salience Structure and Active Salience Field

Salience should be distinguished at two levels.

### Salience Structure

The **salience structure** is the broader weighted organization of the system across time.

It includes:

- habituated routes
- recurring fears
- persistent cares
- inherited priorities
- reward histories
- threat histories
- suppressed but still loaded contents
- identity-linked interpretations
- socially learned penalties and permissions
- default explanations
- emotional grooves
- remembered failures
- remembered shelters
- practiced forms of attention
- practiced forms of avoidance

This broader structure is not fully active at every moment.

A salience structure is more like a weighted terrain than a single command.

It shapes what can become active, but it does not place the whole structure online at once.

### Active Salience Field

The **active salience field** is the subset of the broader salience structure that can currently be:

- reactivated from memory
- held in live comparison
- weighted against present input
- sustained long enough to affect routing
- emotionally or cognitively tolerated
- used to shape action in the current interval

A concise formulation:

> The active salience field is the subset of the broader salience structure that can currently be reactivated from memory and held in live weighting against a given salient input during the bounded interval in which that input is being processed, under present biological, local, and capacity conditions.

This matters because finite systems do not act from their whole structure at once.

They act from what can currently be brought online and kept in play.

A concise formulation:

> The salience structure is the broader terrain. The active salience field is the part of the terrain currently lit enough to route through.

---

## The Moment

In this framework, a **moment** is not an arbitrary unit of time.

A moment is the bounded interval during which a chosen salient input is being processed.

During that interval, some contents can be reactivated and held in the active salience field, while others remain inaccessible, too weak, too costly, too threatening, too suppressed, or too backgrounded to sustain.

This helps explain why the same person may route differently at different times without requiring a total change in deeper structure.

The broader salience structure may remain relatively stable while the active field changes sharply.

A person may still care about a value, plan, relationship, or duty while being unable to hold it online strongly enough in a specific moment.

A concise formulation:

> A moment is the live routing interval of a salient input.

Another:

> Different moments expose different portions of the same salience structure.

## Capacity Is Multi-Dimensional

Salience does not operate over a single scalar resource.

Human systems contain multiple bounded capacities, including but not limited to:

- metabolic energy
- cognitive focus
- attention
- emotional bandwidth
- working memory
- time
- physical recovery capacity
- sensory tolerance
- social trust
- coordination capacity
- executive control
- imaginative range
- pain tolerance
- uncertainty tolerance

Salience stabilizes whenever a dominant capacity is sufficiently occupied to absorb direction, even if other capacities remain underutilized or are being silently depleted.

A system can therefore feel stable, engaged, and coherent while drifting toward delayed failure in a backgrounded capacity.

Salience answers:

> What can absorb capacity now?

Not:

> What preserves long-term viability across all dimensions?

This is why salience can stabilize around paths that are real but costly.

A person may be cognitively absorbed while biologically depleted.

A group may be politically energized while socially exhausted.

A market may be attention-rich while materially ungrounded.

An institution may be procedurally active while legitimacy is draining underneath.

A concise formulation:

> Salience can stabilize the foreground while quietly taxing the background.

---

## Biology, Locality, and Path Dependence

Salience is continuously reshaped by:

- biological state
- current locality
- path dependence
- present load
- reward and threat signaling
- available options
- habituated routes
- social feedback
- environmental affordances
- institutional pressure
- memory reactivation
- recovery debt

This means salience is not static.

A tired, hungry, frightened, humiliated, overstimulated, or overloaded person may not lose their broader salience structure, but they may lose the ability to hold many competing weights inside the active salience field.

A person who is exhausted may still value:

- sleep
- duty
- truth
- social care
- long-term plans
- self-respect
- responsibility
- repair

But they may be unable to actively keep enough of those weights online to compete with the immediate biological sink of rest, shutdown, threat response, or relief-seeking.

So capacity degradation does not mean zero salience.

It means a **narrower active salience field**, fewer live routes, and fewer locally sustainable ends in the moment.

A concise formulation:

> Capacity degradation does not erase salience. It compresses the active field.

Another:

> The field narrows before the person disappears.

## Salience Equilibrium

Salience structure tends toward equilibrium during development and throughout life.

This does not mean it seeks happiness, truth, joy, peace, paranoia, fear, virtue, health, or any other value as its final state.

It means the salience system tends toward an internally stable routing condition in which available capacities can be absorbed, recurring signals can be processed, and the system can continue cohering under current constraints.

A concise formulation:

> Salience does not seek happiness first. It seeks workable internal routing.

Another:

> Salience equilibrium is the system finding a stable way to spend capacity and process signal.

This equilibrium is not necessarily healthy.

A person may stabilize around:

- vigilance
- fear
- resentment
- overwork
- caretaking
- avoidance
- ambition
- ideology
- addiction
- withdrawal
- peace
- curiosity
- play
- trust
- repair

Each can become an equilibrium if it reliably absorbs capacity, organizes expectation, processes signals, and provides enough route stability for the system to continue.

This is why salience equilibrium must not be confused with flourishing.

A harmful equilibrium may still be an equilibrium.

A paranoid field may process the world through threat.

An overworked field may process the world through productivity.

An avoidant field may process the world through withdrawal.

An ideological field may process the world through a master explanation.

A peaceful field may process the world through trust and recovery.

The structural question is not only:

> What does this person value?

It is also:

> What routing equilibrium has this system learned to maintain?

## Development and Equilibrium Formation

Salience equilibrium is shaped by persistence systems and interaction history.

Persistence systems include:

- genetics
- temperament
- nervous-system tendencies
- metabolic limits
- sensory thresholds
- attachment needs
- biological rhythms
- developmental stage
- recovery capacity

Interaction history includes:

- family patterns
- reward histories
- threat histories
- trauma
- schooling
- social roles
- culture
- institutions
- repeated success
- repeated failure
- humiliation
- belonging
- deprivation
- care
- punishment
- environmental stability or instability

The salience structure is therefore not authored from nothing.

It is built through the interaction between inherited persistence systems and accumulated lived contact.

A concise formulation:

> Salience equilibrium is inherited tendency plus lived weighting.

Another:

> Biology gives the field tendencies. Life teaches it routes.

This also means that salience cannot be judged only by the present behavior it produces.

A present route may be the visible surface of a long developmental weighting process.

A person may not be choosing the route from a blank field.

They may be routing through a field already shaped by what repeatedly worked, failed, threatened, rewarded, punished, or stabilized them.

A concise formulation:

> The route is present. The weighting has history.

## Equilibrium and Signal Processing

A salience equilibrium must process signals.

Signals may include:

- hunger
- fatigue
- danger
- affection
- rejection
- shame
- reward
- pain
- boredom
- novelty
- uncertainty
- social approval
- social penalty
- local opportunity
- environmental constraint
- institutional demand
- memory activation
- future expectation

When signals fit the current salience structure, they are processed more easily.

When signals do not fit, the system may experience:

- confusion
- agitation
- denial
- overload
- avoidance
- defensive interpretation
- compulsive explanation
- sudden reweighting
- collapse of direction

A concise formulation:

> A salience field stabilizes when incoming signals can be routed without overwhelming the field.

Another:

> Equilibrium is partly the ability to keep signal from becoming chaos.

Signal processing is not the same as truth processing.

A salience structure may process a signal by accurately integrating it.

It may also process a signal by suppressing it, distorting it, moralizing it, explaining it away, or routing it into an existing pattern.

This is why equilibrium can preserve error.

A concise formulation:

> A field can process a signal without honoring the signal.

---

## Equilibrium Is Dynamic

Salience equilibrium is not a fixed resting point.

It is dynamic.

The system must keep routing capacity while conditions change.

New events, losses, opportunities, relationships, illnesses, institutions, threats, and meanings can disturb equilibrium.

When disturbance occurs, the salience structure may:

- absorb the signal
- suppress the signal
- reinterpret the signal
- route around the signal
- reorganize itself
- collapse temporarily
- form a new equilibrium

A concise formulation:

> Salience equilibrium is not stillness. It is continued routing under changing constraint.

Another:

> The field stays coherent by adjusting what it can absorb, ignore, repeat, or reweight.

This makes salience equilibrium a moving stability rather than a static balance.

It is not a perfect center.

It is a working pattern.

---

## Harmful Equilibria

A salience equilibrium can preserve coherence while damaging long-term viability.

This happens when the system stabilizes around routes that absorb capacity now but produce delayed cost.

Examples:

- workaholism stabilizes capacity through productivity while depleting recovery
- paranoia stabilizes uncertainty through threat detection while damaging trust
- addiction stabilizes distress through immediate relief while narrowing route-space
- ideology stabilizes complexity through explanation while reducing correction
- people-pleasing stabilizes belonging through compliance while eroding agency
- avoidance stabilizes fear through non-contact while shrinking future options
- resentment stabilizes injury through repeated accusation while narrowing repair
- perfectionism stabilizes uncertainty through control while exhausting exploration

A concise formulation:

> A harmful equilibrium is still an equilibrium if it keeps the system coherent while quietly taxing the floor.

Another:

> Stability can be real and still be expensive.

This matters because outside observers may mistake harmful equilibrium for preference, character, laziness, irrationality, or moral failure.

The better question is:

> What function is this equilibrium serving, and what cost does it hide?

---

## Route-Space, Not Just Route Selection

Salience concerns not only the path currently taken.

It also concerns the **reachable route-space**:

- what routes remain visible
- what routes remain emotionally or cognitively reachable
- what routes remain socially or materially viable
- what routes have become too costly
- what routes have been blocked, degraded, or hidden
- how many meaningful sinks remain available
- what routes can be imagined
- what routes can be attempted without immediate collapse
- what routes can absorb capacity long enough to stabilize

This is important because a system may still act while having:

- fewer viable routes
- fewer livable local ends
- less exploratory range
- less recoverability
- stronger funneling into a narrow sink
- fewer ways to process contradiction
- fewer ways to convert signal into correction

So degradation does not require total immobilization.

A concise formulation:

> Less capacity does not mean no direction. It means fewer directions remain livable.

Another:

> A person can keep moving while their route-space is collapsing.

Route-space also explains why advice often fails.

A suggested route may be cognitively understandable while being emotionally, biologically, socially, or materially unreachable in the person's active field.

A concise formulation:

> A route is not available merely because it can be named.

## Habituation and Stabilization

As salient patterns persist, they are reinforced through **habituation**.

Habituation reduces the cost, whether energetic, cognitive, emotional, or social, of repeatedly traversed paths.

When habituated paths align with viable local ends, the system may experience this alignment as:

- reward
- relief
- restoration
- competence
- familiarity
- belonging
- ease
- continuity

When salient paths are disrupted, blocked, or rendered non-viable, the system produces corrective signals such as:

- pain
- fatigue
- distress
- agitation
- dread
- confusion
- irritability
- grief
- shutdown

These signals are not moral judgments.

They are **efficiency feedback**, indicating misalignment between capacity allocation, habituated structure, signal processing, and continued viability.

Habituation can stabilize both healthy and harmful routes.

A route may become easier because it is good for the system.

A route may also become easier because the system has repeated it under constraint.

A concise formulation:

> Habituation lowers routing cost. It does not prove the route is good.

Another:

> Familiarity is not the same as viability.

---

## Salience Without Global Optimization

Salience does not require optimal depletion of all capacities.

A local end may dominate salience by strongly occupying one capacity, such as cognitive abstraction, emotional vigilance, social approval seeking, or physical exertion, even while metabolic exhaustion or recovery debt remains backgrounded.

In such cases:

- direction remains stable
- competing local ends are suppressed
- future-facing constraints may remain non-salient
- delayed costs may accumulate
- the field may feel coherent while the floor is being taxed

This explains sustained periods of intense engagement, whether intellectual, emotional, ideological, relational, political, or occupational, without immediate collapse.

Delayed enforcement is still possible.

A concise formulation:

> Salience can maintain direction without maintaining the whole system.

Another:

> Local coherence is not global viability.

---

## Salience Instability Under Excess Capacity

Salience is shaped not only by scarcity, but also by **capacity surplus**.

Because capacity must be routed, salience requires local ends capable of absorbing it at sufficient rate.

When available capacity per unit time exceeds the absorption capacity of existing salient paths, instability emerges.

This does not require deprivation or conflict.

It can arise when:

- salient paths are frictionless
- local ends are reached too quickly
- sustained engagement requires little effort
- habituation fails to generate durable structure
- novelty is consumed faster than it can stabilize
- option-space expands faster than local ends can organize it
- no available route feels load-bearing enough

In these conditions, local ends cease to stabilize.

Salience reallocates in search of new sinks.

A concise formulation:

> Surplus capacity can destabilize when no local end can absorb it.

Another:

> Too little capacity collapses route-space. Too much unrouted capacity scatters it.

---

## Observable Effects of Excess-Capacity Instability

Common manifestations include:

- restlessness without clear object
- rapid cycling of interests or identities
- escalation of novelty-seeking
- difficulty resting despite adequate resources
- agitation without external threat
- compulsive optimization
- constant reframing
- repeated abandonment of low-friction ends
- searching for difficulty as a stabilizer
- creating conflict to generate salience load

This is often misinterpreted as boredom, pathology, or moral failure.

It is a **rate mismatch** between available capacity and stabilizing absorption.

A concise formulation:

> Boredom can be excess capacity looking for a sink.

Another:

> Restlessness may be salience searching for a load-bearing route.

## Ideas as Local Ends

Local ends are not limited to physical outcomes.

**Ideas themselves can function as local ends.**

Examples:

- thinking and idea exploration
- understanding reality
- optimization
- capacity tracking
- self-analysis
- balance as abstraction
- justice
- purity
- freedom
- repair
- proof
- explanation
- worldview completion
- theoretical coherence

When an idea continuously absorbs attention and cognition, it can dominate salience as effectively as physical activity.

In such cases:

- salience stabilizes within the mental domain
- movement, sociality, and rest may background
- future constraints may remain non-salient
- the idea may become a sink for capacity
- the idea may become an equilibrium anchor

This is not automatically pathology.

It is salience operating normally under constraint.

But idea-local ends can become dangerous when they totalize, detach from correction, or consume capacities they cannot replenish.

A concise formulation:

> An idea can become a place where capacity lives.

Another:

> Thinking is not outside salience. Thinking is one of salience's routes.

## Inherited Salience

Not all salience is freshly authored.

Some salience is **inherited** through:

- family systems
- social roles
- cultural scripts
- institutions
- trauma environments
- prestige hierarchies
- ideological systems
- reward and punishment structures
- dependency arrangements
- economic pressure
- religious formation
- schooling
- class position
- gender expectations
- national myths

This means people often inherit:

- viable sinks
- less viable sinks
- distorted but stabilizing sinks
- socially rewarded sinks that never fully click
- shame-loaded routes
- fear-loaded routes
- forbidden routes
- prestige routes
- inherited local ends
- inherited false constraints

Inherited salience is still real salience.

The route is still routed.

What changes is how the field was shaped before the person moved through it.

A concise formulation:

> Inherited salience is not fake routing. It is pre-shaped routing.

Another:

> The person moves, but the field was already weighted.

## False Constraints and Manipulated Salience

False constraints do not remove salience.

They distort it.

These are often inherited or imposed abstractions treated as if they were hard limits, such as:

- "I must always be productive to deserve rest"
- "Only one path is respectable"
- "Ordinary life is unserious"
- "Deviation is danger"
- "My worth depends on staying on-axis"
- "If I stop, I fail"
- "If I question this, I betray the group"
- "If I disappoint someone, I am unsafe"

These distort the salience field by:

- raising the cost of rival routes
- narrowing perceived option space
- penalizing deviation
- making some sinks appear forbidden or unreachable
- installing background shame or fear
- routing signal into preloaded explanation
- making correction feel like danger
- making rest feel like guilt
- making exit feel like betrayal

Manipulation works through salience, not outside it.

A manipulated action is still saliently routed.

What changes is the weighting of routes, the visibility of sinks, and the shape of the live field.

A concise formulation:

> Manipulation does not replace salience. It distorts the field through which salience routes.

This is also why some manipulations fail.

The same signal does not land equally across all people, because salience structures and active fields differ.

A concise formulation:

> Same signal, different weights, different route.

Another:

> Manipulation succeeds when it finds or installs a route the field can use.

## Balance as a Local End

Balance can function as:

- a stabilizing abstraction
- a coordination heuristic
- an indirect regulator of multiple capacities
- a route-space preserving habit
- a correction signal against totalization
- a way to prevent one local end from eating the field

However, like all local ends, balance can be totalized or distorted.

When moralized or rigidified, it can increase instability rather than reduce it.

A person may become destabilized by trying to maintain balance as a perfect image rather than using it as a living regulator.

Salience does not seek balance as an ideal.

It seeks **viability under constraint**.

Balance is useful when it helps preserve viable routing across capacities.

It becomes harmful when it becomes another sovereign local end.

A concise formulation:

> Balance is a tool of viability, not salience's final god.

Another:

> Balance helps when it keeps routes alive. It harms when it becomes the only route.

---

## Salience Collapse Under Loss of Local Ends

A severe failure mode occurs when highly salient local ends are destroyed or rendered unreachable.

Salience can lose its primary routing structure.

This registers as:

- grief
- disorientation
- aimlessness
- collapse of motivation
- loss of appetite for former routes
- inability to choose
- inability to imagine continuation
- agitation without direction
- shutdown

Capacity remains available.

Paths no longer lead to viable completion.

This is structural rupture, not weakness.

A concise formulation:

> Collapse can occur when capacity remains but the route no longer completes.

Another:

> The field still has energy, but its main sink has disappeared.

---

## Loss as Devaluation of Idea Viability

Loss can invalidate not only a local end, but the surrounding idea-cluster.

Salience may reclassify related ideas as:

- too costly
- too fragile
- unsafe
- likely to reproduce destabilization
- no longer trustworthy
- no longer worth the routing cost
- too exposed to future collapse

Ideas may remain cognitively accessible while no longer attracting salience.

This is **protective reweighting**, not denial.

A concise formulation:

> An idea can remain understood while no longer feeling livable.

Another:

> Loss can make an entire idea-cluster lose routing power.

Protective reweighting may preserve the system in the short term while narrowing route-space in the long term.

## Totalized Local Ends and Future-Space Collapse

Some local ends function as primary organizers of:

- meaning
- identity
- time horizon
- subordinate goals
- future imagination
- social belonging
- self-recognition
- moral orientation
- daily routing
- sacrifice tolerance

When such a totalized end collapses, salience may lose access not only to present stabilizers, but to **imagined futures**.

This registers as:

- loss of future orientation
- inability to imagine viable next states
- a sense that "there is no future"
- collapse of subordinate plans
- loss of meaning continuity
- derealization of former goals
- inability to feel why action matters

This is **future-space collapse**, not indecision.

A concise formulation:

> When a totalized local end collapses, the future can collapse with it.

Another:

> Future-space is not only imagined. It is saliently maintained.

---

## Recovery as Salience Re-Routing

Recovery is not external to salience dynamics.

It is a stabilization outcome following redirection.

When a new path absorbs even minimal capacity without immediate failure, reinforcement begins.

Recovery is:

- iterative
- energy-regulatory
- non-moral
- often gradual
- route-space dependent
- signal-sensitive
- biologically constrained
- habituation-dependent

Over time, recovery itself can habituate and become a default maneuver.

Recovery is not simply the discovery of a better idea.

It is the formation of a new routing equilibrium.

A recovery path must absorb enough capacity to become repeatable.

It must process enough signal to become believable.

It must survive enough contact to become trusted.

This often requires:

- small viable routes
- reduced overload
- new habits
- safer feedback
- corrected false constraints
- restored recovery capacity
- broader route-space
- enough repetition for habituation
- enough success for reinforcement
- enough failure-tolerance to keep the route open

A concise formulation:

> Recovery begins when a new route can absorb capacity without immediate failure.

Another:

> Healing is not only insight. It is re-equilibration.

And another:

> To change a life, it is not enough to change a belief. The salience field must find a new equilibrium it can actually live inside.

---

## Structural Summary

Salience is the bounded routing and weighting of finite capacity.

It:

- directs
- stabilizes
- reinforces
- dissipates
- reactivates
- suppresses
- narrows or widens route-space
- operates through inherited and current weighting
- compresses under degraded capacity
- searches for sinks under surplus capacity
- processes signals through existing structure
- tends toward internal routing equilibrium
- can stabilize around healthy or harmful patterns

It is shaped by:

- biology
- genetics
- temperament
- development
- memory
- interaction history
- reward and threat learning
- current capacity
- available local ends
- environmental constraint
- social feedback
- institutional pressure
- false constraints
- habituated routes

It destabilizes under:

- excess capacity
- loss of local ends
- loss of idea viability
- collapse of totalized ends
- distorted route-space
- false constraints that narrow viable sinks
- signal overload
- capacity degradation
- failure of existing routes to absorb capacity
- failure of incoming signals to fit the current field

It re-stabilizes through:

- iterative redirection
- discovery of new local ends
- habituation of viable paths
- restoration of route-space
- restoration of active field capacity
- corrected false constraints
- new signal-processing patterns
- formation of new routing equilibria

Salience does not seek meaning, virtue, happiness, truth, or optimization in the abstract.

It seeks **workable routing and viability under constraint**.

This means salience equilibrium must not be confused with flourishing.

A harmful equilibrium may still cohere.

A healthy equilibrium must do more than absorb capacity. It must remain answerable to biology, locality, route-space, signal, and future viability.

A concise formulation:

> Salience seeks equilibrium before it seeks goodness.

Another:

> The question is not only what is salient, but what kind of equilibrium the salience field is maintaining.

Another:

> Salience is how finite systems make capacity livable, routable, and coherent under constraint.

Final compression:

> Capacity must flow. Salience routes it. Structure weights it. Equilibrium stabilizes it. Viability tests it.
